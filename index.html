A human-driven framework for therapy

Therapy that travels with you.

Beyond the Room helps therapists extend care into their clients' everyday lives, with tools they choose and shape for each person, in their own voice, on devices the client controls. AI supports the therapist. It never replaces them.

Why

1 hour in the room. 167 beyond it.

Change happens in the rest of the week, yet most therapy still lives inside one hour. People leave sessions with real insight and little support for turning it into practice.

  • Insight fades by midweek.Between-session practice is linked to better outcomes, but it's often a worksheet and a hope.
  • Clients learn self-help alone.Books, apps, and chatbots fill the gap, without a clinician to choose what fits.
  • Therapists carry it all in their heads.History, plans, and patterns live in notes and memory, and preparation competes with presence.

Demand is rising. The therapist's hour is fixed.

Principles

Human-driven, not AI-driven.

Not one big platform of templates. A framework each therapist makes their own, because personalization and clinical judgment are what keep clients safe.

The relationship leads

Tools and AI give the therapist more presence. They never stand in for the person in the room.

Curated, not templated

Therapists choose, adapt, and sequence tools for each client, in their own words and cultural framing.

Teach, don't just treat

Every tool explains the why, so clients build skills and agency they keep long after therapy ends.

Private by design

Nothing leaves the client's device unless they choose to share it, and they always see what is shared.

How it works

One loop, carried by the therapist.

The session stays the heart of the work. Beyond the Room adds the parts that usually go missing.

  1. SessionTherapist and client choose a practice that fits this client, this week.
  2. PracticeShort, visual, guided tools in the client's pocket, in the therapist's voice.
  3. ReflectThe client notices patterns and sees their own progress, saved only on their device.
  4. Share, by choiceThe client decides what to bring back: a summary, a map, a few ratings.
  5. PlanThe therapist arrives prepared, and the next session starts where the week left off.

Scenarios

What it looks like in real life.

Three illustrative stories, two from the client's side and one from the therapist's. Names and details are invented.

A wave of panic on a Tuesday night

Mina, 29, working on panic and the urge to escape

In session

Mina and her therapist practice riding an urge together. They agree on a plan: ride it whenever it shows up this week, and rate how high it gets.

Ride the Wave ↗

During the week

Tuesday, 11pm, the panic rises to 8 out of 10. Instead of leaving, she opens the tool, breathes with the wave, and watches it crest and fall in about four minutes.

Next session

She brings her ratings: peaks dropped from 8 to 5 over the week. They celebrate the evidence and pick the next small step together.

The messages we carry from home

Daniel, 34, a bicultural high achiever who can't stop pushing

In session

Perfectionism keeps coming up. His therapist sends a family map to explore where the pressure began, adapted with Korean family terms.

During the week

He maps Appa, Halmeoni, and the uncle who mentored him. He notices "Make us proud" and rewrites it: "I can be proud of myself, and rest."

Next session

He emails his summary the night before. The session starts from his map, then turns to one thought holding him back.

Design

Privacy by architecture, not by promise.

The client's inner life shouldn't sit in a central database. Today's tools run entirely on the client's device. The direction we're exploring keeps it that way, even as the loop gets richer.

Today Live

Tools run in the client's browser. The client decides what leaves.

  • No accounts, cookies, analytics, or tracking.
  • The client reads and edits the summary in their own email app before sending.
  • Every tool links to 988 and points back to the therapist.

Where we're heading Exploring

A secure pairing between one therapist and one client, with AI that runs on their own devices.

  • Only the paired therapist and client can read what passes between them.
  • The client sees and approves every summary before it is sent.
  • Safety stays human: tools always point to 988 and the therapist.
